Journal article · Published 10 July 2026

Accelerating circular economy education: novel methods, case studies, and integrating AI forecasting and predictions

Kio, P. (2026). Accelerating circular economy education: novel methods, case studies, and integrating AI forecasting and predictions. Frontiers in Sustainability, 7, 1864143. https://doi.org/10.3389/frsus.2026.1864143

Why it matters

The built environment accounts for roughly half of global material extraction and a large share of energy use, emissions, and waste. Yet education for the circular economy is often fragmented, with few chances for students to connect theory, digital tools, and real-world practice.

What we did

Over two fall semesters (2024 and 2025), a new course at the University of Florida used constructivist, project-based, and experiential learning. Students:

  • toured the city recycling facility and selected a non-hazardous industrial waste stream;
  • designed circular building products, reviewed them in immersive virtual reality, and built prototypes;
  • assessed impacts with the MCI and the U.S. EPA Waste Reduction Model (WARM);
  • explored Internet of Things (IoT) sensors and AI tools for waste sorting, waste-volume prediction, and form generation.

In 2026 the course was redesigned as an AI-enabler course, with AI-related content making up about 30–45% of the curriculum.

Three student case studies

Student-led circular product case studies and their Material Circularity Indicator (MCI) scores
Case study Waste stream Building product Conversion ratio MCI score
Footsteps Discarded sneaker soles grown with mycelium Modular garden pavement 2 shoes per pavement unit 1.0
Lotus-weave shingle panel Waste tire rubber, recycled metal wire, cardboard and wood debris Bio-inspired, self-cleaning roofing shingle with IoT sensors 4 shingles per tire 0.90

MCI scores range from 0 (fully linear) to 1 (fully circular).

Key results

  • 241,230 passenger vehicles' worth of annual greenhouse gas emissions potentially avoided by converting waste shoes into Footsteps pavement (WARM estimate)
  • 127.8 million gallons of gasoline potentially conserved
  • 4.08 / 5 overall course rating in Fall 2024
  • 4.92 / 5 graduate-student rating in Fall 2025

The Circular Building Material Avoidance Calculator

To take project results beyond the classroom, a calculator was developed with prompt-based workflows in Google Gemini and published on GitHub. Users pick a U.S. state and a waste stream (shoes, tires, or plastic bottles). The tool estimates how many pavement units, roofing shingles, or wall blocks could be made each month, along with avoided material and MCI scores. A lookup table links each state to its waste-reporting authority so users can check the data.

What students said

“The practical skills gained in AutoCAD, Revit, and Sankey diagram development for material-flow analysis will be highly valuable for my future coursework and professional work.”

Students valued hands-on learning, interdisciplinary teamwork, and the use of quantitative tools. Suggestions for improvement included clearer project goals at the start, a narrower scope, and better computing resources.

Limitations

The study draws on two small cohorts (2 students in 2024 and 12–13 in 2025), so the findings are exploratory. Its main contribution is a transferable teaching design rather than statistically generalizable results.

Conference paper · EAAE–ARCC 2024 Proceedings, pp. 664–671

A circular immersive parametric design workflow: integrating BIM and virtual reality in building design education

Kio, P. (2024). Circular immersive parametric design workflow integrating BIM and VR. In EAAE–ARCC International Conference 2024 Proceedings (pp. 664–671).

Why it matters

The architecture, engineering, and construction (AEC) industry has been slow to adopt Building Information Modeling (BIM) and virtual reality (VR). Game engines such as Unity and Unreal require specialist skills. This study tested a faster route: Revit models viewed in VR through Enscape and a Meta Quest headset.

What we did

Twenty building-design students, working in ten teams, designed an educational office building. Each team designed a parametric, patterned south-facing shade wall meant to replace conventional materials with reused ones and divert waste from landfill. The workflow had four stages:

  1. Material selection for non-load-bearing elements, using the Embodied Carbon in Construction Calculator (EC3) tool
  2. Geometry exploration and parametric unit design in Revit
  3. Two design reviews in immersive VR sessions
  4. Life cycle assessment following ISO 14040/14044, with EPA WARM used to compare virgin and recycled steel

Key findings

  • Thirteen of twenty students (nine of ten teams) took part in VR sessions lasting about 30 minutes per person.
  • While immersed, students voluntarily critiqued their own designs, most often walls, windows, staircases, and building pads, and proposed fixes such as adjusting ceiling heights, moving stairs, and adding windows and handrails.
  • In a user-experience survey, students strongly agreed the VR sessions were new, innovative, interesting, exciting, and enjoyable.
  • Using recycled steel from the city's scrap-metal stream as the secondary material for the shade-wall panels could substantially reduce global warming potential compared with conventional insulated metal panels.

Takeaway: Commercially available tools let students move from 2D spectators to actors inside their own designs, improving spatial understanding while linking design choices to circularity and embodied-carbon outcomes.
